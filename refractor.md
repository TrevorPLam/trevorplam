## 1) Site purpose

The site’s primary job is to function as a professional asset and living knowledge base about Trevor Lam, not as a brochure site. Its core outcome is to help a hiring manager, operator, or founder trust Trevor enough to start a conversation or seriously consider him for an operations-heavy role. A secondary audience is readers who want to follow Trevor’s learning, writing, and long-term growth. ## 2) Positioning

The site should present Trevor as an **Operations Integrator** or **Forensic Stabilizer**: someone who enters broken or high-friction environments, diagnoses the real problem, and stabilizes systems, people, and workflows. That framing is supported by the record across Sonic, Grandlux, and KLW, where Trevor repeatedly improved performance, reduced friction, and took on broader responsibility. The brand should feel calm, precise, and evidence-driven rather than flashy or self-promotional. ## 3) Audience model

The first audience is the evaluator: hiring manager, operator, founder, recruiter, or consultant reading with a “can this person actually do the work?” mindset. The second audience is the builder-reader: someone who may not be hiring immediately but will gain trust by seeing Trevor’s thinking, experiments, and learning logs over time. The site should satisfy both without forcing either group through a generic personal-homepage narrative. ## 4) Content strategy

The content should be divided into proof, explanation, and ongoing learning. Proof is the case studies and capability pages; explanation is the dashboard, philosophy, and skill matrix; ongoing learning is the Lab, Learning Log, Projects, and Archive. This keeps the site from becoming a static resume clone while still making it easy for a busy reader to find evidence quickly. ## 5) Information architecture

The top-level structure should stay stable so the site can grow without redesigning navigation every time new content is added. The recommended main sections are Dashboard, Capabilities, Case Studies, Lab, Resources, and Archive. This is the right level of granularity because it matches how a manager actually explores a candidate: overview first, then function, then proof, then current thinking. The site should feel like a product interface: structured, calm, and analytical. The design language should favor dark surfaces, strong contrast, clear typography, and data-forward components. Metrics should look like metrics, not decorative callouts, because the site’s trust mechanism depends on evidence being visually obvious.
